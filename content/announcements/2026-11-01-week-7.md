**Monday, November 2 · [Week 7: Introduction to Proofs](/weeks/week-07).** The formula $n^2 + n + 41$ gives a prime forty times in a row, and then it doesn't. This week is about why no pile of examples can prove a statement about all numbers, why a single example can destroy one, and how a short, careful argument settles infinitely many cases at once. We cover direct proof, contraposition, contradiction (including why $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational and why the primes never run out), proofs in two directions, proofs by cases, existence proofs and counterexamples. Most importantly, we look at **how to choose** the right technique for the statement in front of you. The notes include a four-panel comic, a **counterexample hunter** that breaks famous claims in your browser, and a **technique chooser** with a proof skeleton for each method. Reading: Rosen 1.7–1.8.

**Lab 6 · [Counterexample Hunting](/labs/lab-06)** opens right after the lecture. You will write a fast prime test (using a theorem from the notes), build the tools of a counterexample hunter, turn claims by Euler, Fermat and Goldbach into Python functions and break them, construct Ramanujan's taxicab number, and meet the Collatz problem, which nobody has solved. Follow the steps in order; each one has an example and the expected result. Every push is graded out of 100, and you can also [submit from the browser](/labs/lab-06#other-ways-to-work-and-submit). **Due: Friday, November 6, 23:59.**
